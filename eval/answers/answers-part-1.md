## 1. Goth Ninja: from memory

~~~text
Goth Ninja Outfit Guide
The era

Goth Ninja is very much a 2012–2015 look, peaking around 2013–2014. It grew out of forums like Styleforum, Superfuture, and Reddit’s r/streetwear, and was pushed into the mainstream by Kanye West’s Yeezus period and A$AP Rocky. Around 2016 it split into techwear (Acronym and its imitators) and quieter “dark minimalism.” It has had a second life since the early 2020s through archive fashion and the Opium/Playboi Carti scene, so it reads as retro-cool now rather than dated.

The rules

The look depends on a few principles more than on any single piece. It’s almost entirely black, with grey or Rick Owens’ off-white “dust” as the only accents. The silhouette is long on top and dropped or tapered below. You layer different lengths so hems peek out under each other. Fabric should drape, logos should be minimal, and the boots or sneakers should be heavy.

The key pieces

1. Elongated tee (base layer). The reference piece is the Rick Owens DRKSHDW Level T. Cheaper versions: longline tees from COS, Uniqlo U, or ASOS. It should hang well past your hips.

2. Asymmetric zip hoodie or draped cardigan (mid layer). The grail is Julius (Tatsuro Horikawa) or a Rick Owens hooded zip with an off-center zipper. Cheaper versions: Zara, ASOS, or AllSaints secondhand.

3. Leather jacket or long drapey coat (outer layer). The grail is the Rick Owens Stooges leather jacket. Boris Bidjan Saberi and Julius are also correct. AllSaints asymmetric leathers are the best value pick, because they were everywhere in this era and resell cheaply.

4. Drop-crotch pants or waxed skinny denim. Grails are Rick Owens drawstring cargos, DRKSHDW Detroit jeans, and Julius gas-mask cargos. Cheaper options are ASOS drop-crotch joggers, Zara jogger-cargos, or black Nike Tech Fleece joggers, which overlapped with the look at the time. Stack the pants over the shoes or cuff them tight.

5. Footwear (the most important piece).

Grail: Rick Owens Geobaskets or Ramones, or Guidi back-zip boots (the 788Z is the classic).
Era-accurate mid-tier: Adidas x Rick Owens collaborations (2013–2017 era, secondhand) or the Y-3 Qasa High.
Budget: triple-black Nike Roshe Run, Adidas Tubular, or black Converse Chuck 70 Hi as a Ramones stand-in.

6. Accessories. Wear a long snood or wrapped scarf and a slouchy elongated beanie. Add Werkstatt:München silver rings or bracelets. Chrome Hearts fits if you want the modern Opium crossover.

Where to buy
New designer: SSENSE (ships to the EU with duties handled), Rick Owens boutiques, and Antonioli, one of the most important stockists for this whole designer circle.
Secondhand (the smart route): Grailed for the US/global archive market, Vestiaire Collective in Europe, plus Depop and Vinted for budget finds and old AllSaints leathers.
High street: COS, Uniqlo, Zara, and ASOS.
Realistic budget
Tier	Approach	Approx. total
Budget	High street plus Vinted/Depop, Chucks or Roshes	€250–450
Mid	DRKSHDW tee and pants, secondhand AllSaints leather, used Adidas x Rick or Ramones	€1,000–1,800
Grail	Mainline Rick or Julius throughout, Stooges jacket, Geobaskets or Guidis	€5,000–8,000+

These prices come from my general knowledge, not current listings, so check actual retail and resale prices before buying, especially for Rick Owens. If you’re going mid-tier, put most of the money into shoes and jacket. Those two pieces carry the whole outfit, and cheap tees and pants are easy to hide underneath them.

## 1. Goth Ninja: with the record

~~~text
Goth Ninja Outfit (2009–2014)

Goth Ninja belongs to roughly 2009 to 2014. It grew out of menswear forums like Styleforum and Superfuture and peaked around 2011–2013. The look is long, dark, thin layers with a lot of drape, slouchy drop-crotch shapes, and chunky designer footwear. Unlike modern techwear, it isn’t about utility or technical fabrics.

The Outfit

Base layer: extra-long draped tee. A long black or dark grey jersey tee from Rick Owens (main line or DRKSHDW) or Julius, falling well past the hips. Thin, slightly sheer jersey is ideal because the look depends on fabric that hangs and moves.

Mid layer: hooded cardigan. A hooded cardigan or draped jersey hoodie from Julius or Damir Doma, cut longer than the tee in places so the layers stagger. Asymmetric hems and wrap fronts are very on-theme.

Outer layer (optional): leather jacket or draped jacket. A Rick Owens leather jacket is the classic choice. For something lighter, a long, unstructured cotton jacket from Damir Doma or 11 by BBS works well. 11 by BBS is Boris Bidjan Saberi’s diffusion line, which arrived late in the era.

Bottoms: drop-crotch shorts over leggings. This is the signature move. Wear Julius or Rick Owens drop-crotch shorts over black leggings or skinny jersey pants. If that feels like too much, drop-crotch trousers from either brand give the same silhouette more quietly.

Footwear: Rick Owens Geobaskets. These high-top sneakers are the defining shoe of the aesthetic. Black leather with a white sole is the canonical version. Alternatives include Rick Owens Ramones or Dunks, or back-zip leather boots in the artisanal style.

Accessories: A long, thin jersey scarf wrapped loosely and minimal silver jewelry. The palette stays black, charcoal, and grey, with maybe some off-white.

Where to Buy

Since this is a past-era look, secondhand is the main route. Grailed is the easiest starting point for Rick Owens, Julius, and Damir Doma pieces from that period. Yahoo Auctions Japan is excellent for Julius in particular, since it’s a Japanese brand. You’ll need a proxy service such as Buyee or ZenMarket to bid and ship internationally. eBay and Vestiaire Collective turn up occasional finds. For current-season Rick Owens that fits the silhouette, Rick Owens stores and major designer retailers still carry draped basics and Geobasket-style shoes.

One sizing note: Julius and other Japanese labels often run small. Check measurements rather than trusting the tag size.

Realistic Budget

These are rough secondhand figures from my own knowledge, so current resale prices may differ:

Piece	Approx. secondhand
Long draped tee	$80–200
Hooded cardigan	$200–450
Drop-crotch shorts	$150–300
Leggings / jersey pants	$60–150
Geobaskets	$350–700
Core outfit total	~$850–1,800
Leather jacket (optional)	add $700–1,500+

That lines up with the $600–$2,500 range in your reference record. The low end means carefully hunted pieces and skipping the jacket. The high end includes a jacket or pieces in great condition. Buying everything new at retail would cost several times more, mostly because of the shoes and any leather.

The essentials are the Geobaskets, the drop-crotch bottoms, and the long draped layers. Get those right and the outfit reads as Goth Ninja even if the rest is budget.

The look combines Korean minimalism with Y2K and soft-grunge touches. The palette is mostly grey, black, off-white, and faded blue, with the odd sage or dusty pink. The silhouettes are deliberately mismatched: shrunken tops over baggy bottoms, sheer layers, ribbons and lacing, and sporty shoes.

Budget

A budget version costs about €200–250. It works if you stick to Uniqlo, Weekday, Bershka, and Pull&Bear and buy the sneakers on sale. Vinted is excellent here, since Acubi pieces and Girbaud jeans turn up secondhand regularly.

A mid-range version costs about €450–600. It mixes one or two real Korean-brand pieces, like an Acubi Club top and a Stand Oil bag, with high-street basics and full-price sneakers.

By now it’s less a viral label and more an absorbed style vocabulary. Shrunken layers, Speedcats, and sheer grey tops are everywhere, without most people calling them “Acubi.” Since I’m answering from memory, prices and availability may have shifted, so check current listings before buying.

## 2. Acubi Style: with the record

Acubi Style Outfit

Era: Acubi is an early-2020s aesthetic. It started taking shape around 2021–2022 and blew up on TikTok and Pinterest in 2022–2023. The name comes from Acubi Club, a Korean label whose layered, muted look gave the trend its identity. Your record’s “2021 – Present” is a fair start date. Since I’m not searching, I can’t confirm how current it still is in late 2026.

The vibe: Thin layers, asymmetry, and a washed-out palette of grey, charcoal, black, off-white, and dusty khaki. Think “subversive basics”: simple pieces cut or layered in slightly odd ways.

The outfit
Base layer: A fitted, sheer or thin ribbed long-sleeve top in grey or off-white, so it shows through the layer on top. Musinsa Standard or a YesStyle basic works fine.
Statement layer: An asymmetric one-shoulder knit or a cropped charcoal bolero over the base. This is where Hyein Seo or Acubi Club pieces shine, since both do deliberately “off” knitwear.
Arm warmers: Grey or black ribbed knit, worn over or under the base sleeve. They’re cheap and the most recognizable Acubi signal.
Bottoms: Washed grey or olive low-rise parachute pants with toggles and some distressing. Thug Club suits the grungier version. Depop is great for secondhand pairs.
Shoes: Chunky grey/silver sneakers (New Balance 530s or Asics Gel-1130s are common picks) or simple black platform boots.
Accessories: Chunky silver jewelry, a small nylon shoulder bag, and optionally a slouchy beanie or wired headphones for the full look.
Where to buy

Musinsa (the global site ships internationally) is the best one-stop shop for Korean indie labels. YesStyle covers inexpensive basics and arm warmers. Depop is ideal for secondhand parachute pants and Y2K pieces. You can buy from Acubi Club directly, and W Concept carries more Korean designer brands.

Realistic budget

Your record’s $100–$400 range is realistic only if you skip the headline designers. Approximate tiers, based on general price knowledge rather than current listings:

Budget (~$150–$250): Musinsa Standard and YesStyle basics, Depop pants, mid-range sneakers.
Mid-range (~$300–$500): One real Korean indie piece, like an Acubi Club knit, with basics around it.
Designer (~$800–$1,500+): Hyein Seo and Thug Club are luxury-adjacent. A single knit or jacket can cost several hundred dollars, and Thug Club’s leather pieces go well beyond that, so one item could use up the dataset’s whole budget.

The best value is to spend on the statement layer (the asymmetric knit or bolero) and go cheap on everything else. Since the base, arm warmers, and pants mostly read as texture and color, nobody can tell they’re inexpensive.

## 3. Opium: from memory

~~~text
The Opium Outfit

The Opium look comes from Playboi Carti’s Opium label and its roster: Ken Carson, Destroy Lonely, and Homixide Gang. It’s dark, aggressive, and avant-garde, mixing Rick Owens silhouettes, punk and goth references, heavy silver jewelry, and a vampiric, all-black mood.

Which years it belongs to

The label formed around 2019. The aesthetic really took shape with Carti’s Whole Lotta Red in December 2020. It peaked from roughly 2021 to 2024, carried by Ken Carson’s Project X and X, Destroy Lonely’s If Looks Could Kill, and Ken’s A Great Chaos. By 2025 it had spread well beyond the fanbase into mainstream streetwear, so in 2026 it reads less like a niche subculture and more like an established style. The core pieces haven’t dated, though, because they come from designers who were doing this long before Opium.

The outfit, piece by piece

Shoes: Rick Owens. These are the anchor of the look.

DRKSHDW Ramones. These are the high-top canvas sneakers with the oversized toe cap. They’re the entry point and the most recognizable Opium shoe. Expect to pay about €500–600 new, or €200–350 used.
Mainline Geobaskets or Jumbo Laced sneakers. These are the grail version, at about €1,100–1,500 new and €500–900 used.

Bottoms: stacked or cargo, nothing slim and clean.

Rick Owens DRKSHDW Creatch cargo or Bauhaus cargo pants. These run about €500–700 new.
Alternatives. Flared leather pants, or heavily stacked black or distressed denim, work too. Look at Vetements, Enfants Riches Déprimés, or vintage flares.

Top: layered, oversized, or punk.

Base layer. Wear a black long-sleeve, a Rick Owens Level T (about €250), or a cropped and distressed vintage band or metal tee. Thrifted tees are completely legitimate here.
Hoodie option. Oversized pieces from Balenciaga (about €800–1,000), Vetements, or Raf Simons fit the look.

Outerwear: leather is king.

Rick Owens leather jacket. The Stooges or Intarsia styles run about €2,500–3,500 new and €1,000–1,800 used.
Budget route. A vintage black biker jacket with a good fit costs about €80–250 on secondhand sites.

Jewelry and accessories: this is where the look becomes “Opium.”

Chrome Hearts. Look for cross pendants, Forever rings, Floral Cross pieces, and the trucker hat. Rings run about €500–1,500 each, pendants €1,000+, and trucker hats about €400–600 resale. Fakes are everywhere, so buy from authenticated sellers.
Vivienne Westwood orb pearl necklace. The Bas Relief or Orb Pearl styles were hugely associated with the scene and cost about €250–450.
Extras. A studded belt, a black balaclava or beanie, and sunglasses (Chrome Hearts eyewear if you’re going all in) finish it off.
Where to buy

For new pieces, try SSENSE, Mytheresa, END., and Rick Owens’ own stores and website. Chrome Hearts sells only through its own boutiques, and those sell out constantly. For secondhand, Grailed is the best place for Rick and Chrome Hearts. Vestiaire Collective is good in Europe and has an authentication service. Depop and Vinted work well for vintage leather and band tees. Rick Owens also runs seasonal sample sales and outlet stock, and they’re worth watching.

Realistic budget
Entry level (about €400–800): Used DRKSHDW Ramones, a vintage leather jacket, thrifted band tees, flared or stacked jeans from secondhand sites, and a Vivienne Westwood necklace. This gets you 80% of the look.
Committed (about €2,000–4,000): New Ramones or used Geobaskets, Rick cargos, a Level T, a used Rick or quality leather jacket, and one real Chrome Hearts ring.
Full grail (€8,000–15,000+): Mainline Rick footwear and leather, Balenciaga or ERD layers, and a stacked Chrome Hearts set with pendant, rings, and hat.

Prices are approximate and shift with resale demand, especially for Chrome Hearts. My tip is to spend first on the shoes and the silhouette (baggy, stacked, dark), then add jewelry over time. A cheap fit with real Ramones and the right proportions looks more Opium than an expensive fit that’s styled too clean.

## 4. Ura-Hara (Harajuku 90s): from memory

~~~text
Ura-Hara Outfit: Harajuku Back Streets, 1993–2003

The era: Ura-Hara means “behind Harajuku,” the small side streets away from Omotesando. The scene took shape in the early 90s around Hiroshi Fujiwara and his label Goodenough (1990). It took off in 1993 when Nigo and Jun Takahashi opened the shop NOWHERE, with Bape on one side and Undercover on the other. The peak was roughly 1995–2000, carried by magazines like Boon and Smart, tiny production runs, and lines outside stores. By the mid-2000s the brands had gone global (Bape’s Pharrell years, Undercover showing in Paris), and the underground part ended. So the core is about 1993–2003.

The look mixes American skate and hip-hop clothes, military surplus, punk graphics, and obsessive Japanese quality, with very limited releases.

The Outfit
Jacket: A Bape 1st Camo M-65 or coach jacket, or a WTAPS/Neighborhood military jacket if you’d rather avoid loud logos. Military outerwear is the most typical Ura-Hara layer.
Top: A graphic tee from Undercover, Goodenough, or Bape (the ape-head or college logos are classics). Graphic tees were the scene’s main currency, so this is the most important piece.
Bottoms: Vintage Levi’s 501s or loose straight-leg fatigue pants (Neighborhood and WTAPS both make good ones), cuffed or a little stacked. Not skinny.
Shoes: Nike Air Max 95 in the “neon” colorway, which caused a buying frenzy in Japan in 1995–97 known as “Air Max hunting.” Air Force 1s or Jordan 1s are also accurate. Bape Stas came later (2002), right at the end of the era.
Bag: A Porter Tanker shoulder bag or Head Porter. Porter’s collaborations with Fujiwara are central to the period.
Watch: A Casio G-Shock, ideally a DW-5600 or DW-6900. G-Shock collaborations were huge.
Optional: A beanie or bucket hat, a Bounty Hunter or Medicom vinyl toy on your desk, and, if money is no object, Goro’s silver feather jewelry, the scene’s ultimate status piece.
Where to Buy

Archive (90s originals):

Japanese marketplaces through proxy services like Buyee, ZenMarket, or FromJapan. These give you access to Yahoo Auctions Japan and Mercari Japan, where the most stock is and prices are often lowest.
Japanese resale chains such as 2nd Street and Ragtag.
Grailed, Vinted, and eBay.

New production (Bape, Neighborhood, WTAPS, and Undercover are all still active):

Brand stores; Bape has European locations.
Stockists such as END., HBX, and Slam Jam.

Watch out: Bape is among the most counterfeited brands ever. Check tags, printing quality, and seller history, and ask for detailed photos.

Realistic Budget (EUR)
Approach	Total	How
Inspired by the era	€300–500	Vintage 501s (€50–80), one current brand tee (€80–120), Air Max 95 or AF1 (€120–190), G-Shock (€100)
Real mix	€900–1,600	Add a Bape or Neighborhood jacket (€300–500 new or used), a Porter Tanker (€250–350), and one archive tee (€100–250)

The most effective tip: put your money into one strong authentic piece, like an archive tee or a camo jacket, and fill in the rest with vintage and sneakers. Mixing high-end and casual pieces is exactly what the original scene did.

## 5. Health Goth: from memory

~~~text
A Health Goth Outfit

The era: Health Goth belongs to roughly 2013–2016. It came out of Portland around 2013–2014, when a few people from the electronic music scene (members of Magic Fades and Father Figures) started a Facebook group that collected moody, glossy images of black sportswear. The style peaked in 2014–2015, when fashion and music media picked it up. By about 2017 it had been absorbed into mainstream athleisure and techwear. If you wear it today it reads as a deliberate mid-2010s reference, which is part of the fun.

The formula: all black, with white or reflective accents. It mixes gym and performance gear with a dark, slightly dystopian mood: mesh, compression fabrics, neoprene, technical nylon, chunky sneakers, and wraparound shades. The joke at its core was treating fitness gear the way goths treat lace and leather.

The Outfit, Piece by Piece

Base layer: compression top. A black long-sleeve compression shirt from Nike Pro or Under Armour HeatGear, worn as a visible layer under mesh or with the sleeves showing. It costs about €30–45. Decathlon’s Kiprun or Domyos lines do the same job for €10–20.

Mid layer: mesh or tech hoodie. The Nike Tech Fleece hoodie in black is the obvious modern choice at around €120–140. A black mesh jersey or basketball top layered over the compression shirt is more period-accurate and more Health Goth.

Bottoms: track pants or the leggings-plus-shorts stack. You have two options. Cuffed Y-3 track pants (around €250–350) are the designer version, and Nike Tech Fleece joggers (about €100–120) are the accessible one. The more committed look is black compression tights with nylon running shorts over them, from Nike or Decathlon, for roughly €40–80 total.

Outerwear: technical shell. Look for a black windbreaker or anorak with reflective details. Nike ACG, Adidas Terrex, or Salomon cost around €120–250. Arc’teryx in black is about €400–650 and leans toward gorpcore, but it fits.

Shoes: chunky, all-black runners. This is the most important piece:

The Y-3 Qasa was the defining Health Goth sneaker. It’s easiest to find secondhand, at about €100–250 depending on condition.
Nike Air Max 97 or VaporMax in triple black cost around €180–220.
Nike Air Huarache in triple black is the budget pick at around €120–140.

## 5. Health Goth: with the record

~~~text
Health Goth Outfit: Sterile Gym Dystopia

Era: Health Goth belongs roughly to 2013–2017, with its peak around 2014–2015, when fashion press picked it up and Alexander Wang’s H&M collaboration brought the look to the mainstream. Two small notes on your reference record. The term usually traces back to a Portland-based Facebook page around 2012, which then spread through Tumblr and Instagram, so “born on Tumblr” is a slight simplification. Vaporwave was a neighbouring internet subculture rather than core Health Goth music, which leaned more toward dark electronic, industrial, and club sounds like Arca and Gesaffelstein.

The Pieces

Top: neoprene or scuba-knit hoodie. The Alexander Wang x H&M (2014) scuba hoodies and sweatshirts are close to the definitive Health Goth garment: performance fabric, boxy cut, all black, often with a rubberized logo. Layer a black mesh or perforated athletic tee underneath for that locker-room-meets-lab texture.

Bottom: compression tights under shorts. Black Nike Pro compression tights with loose black running or basketball shorts over them. This layering is the signature Health Goth silhouette. Black joggers from Y-3 or Nike Tech Fleece work as an alternative.

Footwear: chunky black trainers. The Y-3 Qasa High is the era-perfect pick: futuristic, sock-like, and very 2014. Triple-black Nike Air Max 95s or adidas Ultraboosts are easier to find alternatives.

Accessories: the sterile finishing touches. A black athletic visor or blank black cap, Nike Elite crew socks, and optionally a sports harness, sweatbands, or a sleek black water bottle carried as a deliberate accessory. Keep everything matte black, white, or reflective. No colour.

Designer option: Cottweiler pieces if you want the more conceptual, high-fashion end of the aesthetic. They’re harder to find and pricier.

Where to Buy

Grailed is best for Y-3, Cottweiler, and Alexander Wang x H&M. eBay is good for Qasas and old Nike stock. Depop is great for cheaper compression gear, visors, and H&M collab pieces. Basics like compression tights, socks, and shorts are fine to buy new straight from Nike.

Realistic Budget

Mesh tee	$15–30	$50–100

That lands in or near the reference record’s $150–$700 range. A carefully thrifted fit sits around $250–350; going designer on the hoodie and shoes pushes you toward the top.

The key rule is the one your record flags: it should read as gym clothes, not techwear. No cargo pockets, straps-for-utility, or tactical shells. Everything should look like it belongs in a minimalist, slightly unsettling fitness facility.
