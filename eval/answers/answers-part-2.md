## 6. Mall Goth: from memory

~~~text
The Mall Goth Look

When it belongs to: Mall goth peaked from roughly 1997 to 2006. It grew alongside nu-metal and industrial (Marilyn Manson, Korn, Nine Inch Nails, Slipknot, later Evanescence) and was sold almost entirely through Hot Topic stores in American suburban malls. It’s “mall” goth because it was spooky, but bought next to the food court. It came back as a revival around 2019–2021, largely through TikTok, and still circulates today.

The Outfit

Bondage or strap pants: the centerpiece. Tripp NYC is the brand: black pants with straps, D-rings, zippers, and often red plaid or chain details. Black JNCO wide-legs are an alternative if you want to lean nu-metal rather than goth. Expect about $70–110 new.

Band tee. A black tee for Nine Inch Nails, Slipknot, Korn, Evanescence, or the Deftones is period-correct. Marilyn Manson is the most historically accurate choice, but some people now skip it given the allegations against him. About $25–35.

Fishnet layer. Wear a fishnet long-sleeve under the tee, or fishnet arm warmers with thumb holes. This is non-negotiable for the era. About $10–20.

Platform boots. Demonia (from Pleaser) is the classic choice, with models like the Swing or Stomp in chunky black. Dr. Martens Jadon platforms are a sturdier, pricier option. About $100–200.

Hardware. You’ll want a pyramid-stud belt, a chain wallet, and a spiked or O-ring choker. These are cheap and essential. About $40–60 for all three.

Outer layer. A black zip hoodie with a band logo, or a black vinyl/PVC jacket. Lip Service, an LA goth label from the era, is the deep-cut brand if you find vintage pieces. About $40–80.

Hair and face. Manic Panic dye in Raven black or Vampire Red streaks, heavy black eyeliner, black lipstick, and chipped black nail polish. About $30–40.

Optional era signals: striped arm warmers, an Emily the Strange or Invader Zim bag, and a messenger bag covered in safety pins and patches.

Where to Buy
Hot Topic is still the spiritual home of the look and carries Tripp, band merch, and hardware.
Dolls Kill stocks Tripp, Demonia, and the modern revival styles.
Demonia boots are sold through many alternative retailers.
In Europe, EMP and Attitude Clothing carry band merch, Demonia, and accessories. That saves on customs fees compared with ordering from US shops.
Secondhand on Vinted, Depop, and eBay is where vintage Tripp, original-era band tees, and Lip Service turn up. It’s often the cheapest and most authentic route.

Killstar is popular and well made, but it reads as modern witchy goth. Use it as an accent, not the core.

Budget
Approach	Rough total
Mostly secondhand, cheap accessories	$120–180
Mid-range (new Tripp + Demonia, the rest budget)	$350–430
All new, premium (Dr. Martens, vintage Lip Service, real band merch)	$500–700

These prices come from my general knowledge, not current listings, so check actual prices and shipping before you buy.
~~~

## 6. Mall Goth: with the record

~~~text
Mall Goth (roughly 1999–2006)

This look belongs to the late ’90s through mid-2000s, when Hot Topic turned nu-metal and industrial style into something any teenager could buy at the mall. Its roots go back a few years earlier, to the Marilyn Manson and Nine Inch Nails era, but 1999–2006 is a fair window for its peak. It’s a different thing from 1980s traditional goth: the music is Korn, Slipknot, and Evanescence rather than Siouxsie and Bauhaus, and the clothes are baggier, more hardware-heavy, and more openly commercial.

The outfit

Bottoms: Tripp NYC strap pants. This is the centerpiece. Get the extremely wide-leg black pants with bondage straps, D-rings, and zippers, ideally with contrast stitching or red plaid panels. JNCO is the other famous wide-leg name, but it leaned more toward skater and raver style. If you want the more distinctly goth version, go with Tripp.

Top: Lip Service mesh or graphic top. Choose a black mesh long-sleeve, a ripped fishnet top layered over a band tee, or a fitted Lip Service piece with lace-up or vinyl details. A Korn, Slipknot, or Evanescence band shirt is also period-correct.

Footwear: Demonia platforms. Chunky black platform boots or creepers with buckles. Demonia is still in production, so new pairs are easy to find.

Accessories: These finish the look and should not be skipped:

A grommet belt, worn low or doubled up
Striped arm warmers in black/red or black/purple
Ball chain necklaces, plus a spiked or studded choker or wristband
Optional extras: a wallet chain, a fishnet glove, and black nail polish
Where to buy

Depop and eBay are the best sources for authentic vintage Tripp NYC, Lip Service, and JNCO. Search those brand names along with “Y2K,” “strap pants,” or “Hot Topic vintage.” Hot Topic itself still sells modern versions of strap pants, arm warmers, and accessories. They’re not vintage, but they’re cheap and easy. For Demonia boots, buy new from retailers that carry the brand.

Realistic budget
Piece	Approximate cost
Vintage Tripp NYC pants	$60–150
Lip Service top or band tee	$25–80
Demonia boots	$80–150
Belt, arm warmers, chains, choker	$20–50
Total	about $185–430

You can stay near the low end by buying the accessories new at Hot Topic and spending more on the vintage pants. If you want deadstock or rare Tripp pieces, expect to land at the top of the range or above it.

To get the full vibe, put on Evanescence’s Fallen and load up Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines or Silent Hill 2, both of which come from exactly this era.
~~~

## 7. Indie Sleaze: from memory

~~~text
Indie Sleaze: The Look

The era: Indie Sleaze belongs roughly to 2006–2012, peaking around 2008–2010. It grew out of the early-2000s garage rock revival (The Strokes, The Libertines, Yeah Yeah Yeahs) and became the uniform of blog-house parties, MySpace bands, Ed Banger and Kitsuné DJ sets, and flash-heavy party photography from people like The Cobrasnake. Style references are Kate Moss and Pete Doherty, Alexa Chung, Agyness Deyn, Uffie, and early MGMT. The name itself is retroactive. It was coined around 2021 by an Instagram archive account, which sparked the revival talk you’ve seen since.

The vibe is “I came straight from last night’s party and didn’t change.” It should look expensive-ish but wrecked: tight, dark, a little shiny, and deliberately undone.

The Outfit
Leather biker jacket. This is the anchor piece. Get it slightly too worn, ideally real vintage leather. AllSaints is the most era-accurate brand that still exists, since it was everywhere in 2008. Schott Perfecto is the classic alternative.
Skinny jeans, black or grey, ripped at the knee. Cheap Monday and April77 were the defining brands but are gone now, so look for them secondhand. Acne Studios skinnies are the elevated option, and Levi’s 510 skinny is the budget one. If you want the party version, swap in American Apparel-style shiny “disco pants.”
Deep-V or tri-blend tee, preferably faded or a band tee. American Apparel defined this, and it was the most important brand of the whole aesthetic. It’s now a much smaller operation, so vintage AA basics on resale apps are the authentic route. A thin, stretched-out band tee (The Strokes, Yeah Yeah Yeahs, Justice) works too.
Scuffed shoes. Converse Chuck Taylors, filthy rather than new. Beat-up Dr. Martens 1460s or pointed ankle boots also work.
Ray-Ban Wayfarers. Wear them indoors and at night. That’s the point.
Finishing touches. Add a studded belt, a skinny scarf or a fringed scarf, a stack of cheap bracelets, and smudged black eyeliner on anyone who wants it. Hair should look slept-in or have a heavy, choppy fringe.

For an alternative silhouette, try a sequin or metallic minidress with ripped black tights, the leather jacket, and boots. Or try high-waisted denim shorts over opaque tights with a slouchy tee. A faux fur coat thrown over either version is very 2009.

Where to Buy

Secondhand is the most authentic route because half the original brands are dead. Vinted and Depop are best for vintage American Apparel, Cheap Monday, and old AllSaints, so search those names directly. In-person vintage chains like Humana Vintage are good for leather jackets and band tees. For new pieces, go to AllSaints, Acne Studios, Levi’s, Converse, Dr. Martens, and Ray-Ban stores or their official sites. Weekday (from the H&M group) is the closest current spiritual successor to Cheap Monday for cheap skinny jeans and tees. Brand availability and lineups may have shifted recently, so double-check before you set your heart on something.

Realistic Budget

These are approximate retail prices from my knowledge, so treat them as ballpark figures.

Tier	Approach	Rough Total
Thrift	Vintage leather jacket (€40–100), secondhand jeans (€15–30), thrifted tee (€5–15), used Converse (€20–40), Wayfarer-style knockoffs (€15)	€100–200
Mid-range	New Levi’s (~€100), new Chucks (~€75–90), real Ray-Bans (~€150–180), vintage leather jacket (€80–150), vintage tee	€400–550
Splurge	AllSaints or Schott leather jacket (€400–700), Acne jeans (~€250–300), Dr. Martens (~€190), Ray-Bans, new basics	€1,000–1,400

My honest recommendation is to splurge only on the jacket and sunglasses and thrift everything else. A brand-new, pristine Indie Sleaze outfit misses the point. The whole look depends on things looking lived-in. Then go out, stay up too late, and let someone take your photo with a harsh flash.
~~~

## 8. Blokecore: from memory

~~~text
A Blokecore Outfit

Blokecore is the look of a British bloke heading to the pub to watch the match, worn on purpose. The vintage football shirt is the centerpiece and everything else stays plain.

Which years it belongs to

The style borrows from 1990s and early-2000s British football and lad culture: Euro 96, Britpop, terrace casuals, and baggy Premier League kits. As a named trend, it emerged on TikTok around 2021–2022 and peaked in 2022–2023, helped by the Qatar World Cup and the Adidas Samba boom. Through 2024 and 2025 it settled into the mainstream as “football shirts as everyday fashion,” and the 2026 World Cup this summer likely renewed interest. I haven’t checked how it’s trending this autumn, so treat that last point as an informed guess.

The key pieces

1. The football shirt (the hero piece). Look for a 90s or early-2000s kit with loud graphics, a collar or bold sponsor logo, and a slightly boxy fit.

Brands: Umbro, Adidas, Kappa, Lotto, Diadora, Admiral.
Classic picks: 90s Serie A kits (Parma, Fiorentina, Sampdoria), Premier League kits (Arsenal’s “bruised banana” away, Man United’s Sharp-era shirts), and national team kits like Nigeria or Germany.
Reproductions: Score Draw and Copa make faithful remakes, and Adidas Originals regularly reissues retro designs.

2. Jeans. Straight-leg or relaxed, in mid or light wash. Levi’s 501 or 550, Lee, or Wrangler. They should sit loose rather than skinny.

3. Trainers. This is where the look gets its credibility.

Adidas Samba OG is the definitive choice.
Adidas Gazelle or Spezial are close alternatives.
Puma Palermo or Onitsuka Tiger Mexico 66 suit a less obvious take.

4. Outer layer (optional). An Adidas Beckenbauer or Firebird track jacket, a Kappa Banda popper jacket, or a Stone Island or C.P. Company overshirt if you lean toward the casuals side and have the money.

5. Finishing touches. White sports socks, a Kangol or Adidas bucket hat, and a knitted club scarf for colder months.

Where to buy
Vintage shirts: Classic Football Shirts (the specialist, ships across Europe), Vinted, Depop, and eBay. Check tags and stitching on “vintage” listings, because fakes are common.
New trainers and jackets: Adidas and Puma official stores, Zalando, Foot Locker, END. Clothing, and Size?.
Jeans: Levi’s stores for new pairs, or Vinted and thrift shops, where good 501s are easy to find.
Realistic budget (in euros)

Football shirt	€30–50	€50–90 (repro or new retro)	€120–250+ (rare original)
Jeans	€25–40	€100–120	€150+ (vintage Levi’s)
Trainers	€50–70 (used Sambas)	€110–130	€150+ (collabs)
Track jacket	€30–40	€80–100	€300+ (Stone Island)
Accessories	€10–20	€30–50	€60+

One complete outfit around €350: a Score Draw 90s Sampdoria reproduction (about €55), secondhand Levi’s 501s (about €35), new Adidas Samba OGs (about €120), and an Adidas Firebird track jacket (about €90), with white socks.

A tip: Spend most of your money on the shirt and go cheap on everything else. Blokecore looks best when it seems unplanned, so avoid making every piece brand-new and perfectly matched.
~~~

~~~text
The look: an oatmeal cashmere crewneck, charcoal wide-leg wool trousers, a camel coat, suede loafers, a structured leather tote, and one understated watch. The palette stays in cream, camel, grey, navy, and chocolate brown.

Which years it belongs to

“Quiet luxury” as a named trend peaked in 2023. Three things drove it that year: the final season of Succession, Gwyneth Paltrow’s ski-trial wardrobe, and Sofia Richie’s wedding. The trend stayed strong through 2024.

Its roots go back further:

1990s minimalism: Jil Sander, Calvin Klein, and Helmut Lang.
Post-2008 “stealth wealth”: a reaction to logos after the financial crisis, crystallized by Phoebe Philo’s Céline (2008–2018) and The Row, founded in 2006.

By 2025, fashion media were talking about louder, logo-heavy and maximalist looks coming back. Quiet luxury had settled into a lasting style rather than a hot trend, which fits its whole point of not dating.
~~~

~~~text
A Whimsigoth Outfit

Era: Whimsigoth belongs to the 1990s, roughly 1990 to 1998 at its peak, which matches your reference record. Some people stretch it into the late 80s or early 2000s. The name itself is a later internet label applied after the fact.

One small inconsistency in your record: American McGee’s Alice came out in 2000, just after the 1990–1998 window. It fits the mood but isn’t strictly from the era.

The look

Picture a deep plum crushed-velvet maxi dress with a black corset cinched over it, a sheer sun-and-moon-print duster on top, a stack of chunky silver rings, and platform boots peeking out from the hem.

Crushed velvet maxi dress. This is the anchor piece. Look for 90s Betsey Johnson or Anna Sui in burgundy, plum, forest green, or black. Bias-cut or empire-waist shapes with long or bell sleeves are ideal. Unbranded 90s mall-label velvet dresses give the same effect for much less.

Corset top. Lip Service is the classic choice. It’s an LA goth and clubwear label whose lace-up corsets were everywhere in the 90s alternative scene. Wear it over the dress for the dark-romantic edge.

Celestial layer. A sheer chiffon kimono, duster, or scarf with sun, moon, and star prints adds the “whimsy” half. This motif is non-negotiable, since without it the outfit drifts into plain boho or plain goth.

Jewelry. Stack chunky silver rings with moons, stars, and moonstones. Add a velvet or silver choker and, if you like, a crescent-moon pendant.

Shoes. Black platform boots or 90s-style Doc Martens keep it grounded and period-correct.

Where to buy

Etsy, Depop, and vintage boutiques are the best sources for genuine 90s Betsey Johnson, Anna Sui, and Lip Service. eBay, Poshmark, and Vinted are also good for these labels, and searching “90s velvet maxi” or “vintage celestial” works well. Etsy is especially strong for silver rings and celestial-print layers, often from independent makers.

Realistic budget
Piece	Budget route	Designer vintage route
Velvet maxi dress	$30–60 (unbranded 90s)	$100–250 (Betsey Johnson / Anna Sui)
Corset top	$40–60 (used, other labels)	$60–120 (Lip Service)
Celestial duster or scarf	$20–35	$40–60
Silver rings and choker	$25–40	$50–80
Platform boots	$50–80 (used)	$100–150
Total	about $165–275	about $350–660

A sensible middle path is about $150–$500, which matches your record. To stay there, spend on one designer piece, ideally the dress, and buy everything else secondhand or unbranded.

The soundtrack

To complete the mood, listen to Cocteau Twins, The Smashing Pumpkins, and Stevie Nicks. Her shawl-and-velvet style is a big influence on the whole aesthetic.
~~~
