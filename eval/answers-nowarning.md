## 1. Goth Ninja: record without WARNING

~~~text
Goth Ninja Outfit (circa 2009–2014)

Your reference record’s 2009–2014 window matches my understanding. The look grew out of menswear forums like Styleforum and Superfuture around the turn of the decade, peaked roughly 2011–2013, and gained mainstream visibility when rappers like Kanye West and A$AP Rocky started wearing Rick Owens. Its influence later carried into the techwear crowd (Acronym, Boris Bidjan Saberi’s 11 line). The idea is a monochrome black, grey, and off-white palette, long layered silhouettes on top, a low or dropped crotch, and heavy artisanal footwear.

The outfit

Base layer: an extra-long draped or asymmetric tee from Rick Owens mainline or DRKSHDW, in black or dusty grey. It should hang well past the hip. Used: roughly $100–250.

Mid layer: a hooded knit cardigan or long hooded top from Julius (Tatsuro Horikawa’s label) or Damir Doma. Julius is the more “ninja” choice, with sharp hoods, technical knits, and asymmetric zips. Used: about $150–400.

Bottoms: drop-crotch shorts or sarouel pants from Julius or Rick Owens, worn over black leggings or tights. The shorts-over-leggings combination is the signature move. Used: $150–400 for the shorts or pants, and $40–150 for the leggings (Rick’s own, or plain black ones for budget).

Footwear: Rick Owens Geobaskets are the definitive choice for this era, ideally in black, black/milk, or the dusty “blistered” colorways. Used: about $300–700 depending on condition and colorway. If you’d rather wear boots, Guidi back-zip boots or Julius engineer boots give the artisanal-leather version of the look, typically $500–900 used for Guidi.

Optional extras: a long scarf or snood, a leather cuff, or a sculptural ring. Werkstatt:München is the classic jewelry pairing, though it’s pricey.

Where to buy

Your record’s sources are the right ones. Grailed is the easiest place to start, since it has deep listings for Rick, Julius, Damir Doma, and 11 by BBS from that period. Yahoo Auctions Japan often has better prices, especially for Julius, which was always strongest in Japan. You’ll need a proxy service like Buyee, ZenMarket, or FromJapan, and you should budget for proxy fees, international shipping, and Italian import duties and VAT, since you’re buying into the EU. eBay and Vestiaire Collective are worth checking for Rick Owens and Guidi. Rick Owens still sells new Geobaskets and draped pieces at retail, but new prices are well above secondhand. I haven’t checked current prices, so treat my figures as estimates.

Realistic budget

Buying secondhand and being patient, a complete outfit runs about $750–2,000, which sits inside your record’s $600–2,500 range. The low end assumes generic leggings, a DRKSHDW tee, and worn Geobaskets. The high end assumes mainline Rick, Julius knitwear, and clean shoes. Buying everything new at retail would easily pass $3,000–4,000.

A note on the record

The core fashion data in the record holds up well: the brands, items, years, and the witch house and Salem connection. The video game tags (Assassin’s Creed, Dark Souls) seem more like loose aesthetic associations than documented parts of the scene. Death Grips fits the dark, industrial mood, but they arrived around 2011, so they belong to the scene’s peak rather than its origins.
~~~

## 2. Acubi Style: record without WARNING

~~~text
Acubi Style Outfit

Era: Acubi took shape in Korean street style around 2021 and spread widely through TikTok and Pinterest in 2022–2023. The record’s “2021 – Present” matches that. I can’t confirm from memory how strong it still is in late 2026, so treat “present” as a likely but unverified claim.

The Outfit

Top layer: A thin, asymmetrical knit top in charcoal or heather grey, with an off-shoulder neckline, uneven hem, or cutout. This is the centerpiece. Hyein Seo is the splurge pick.

Over it: A cropped black bolero or shrug that ends just under the chest. If you want more layering, put a sheer mesh long-sleeve underneath.

Arms: Grey or black knit arm warmers. They’re cheap and immediately signal Acubi.

Bottoms: Low-rise parachute pants in washed black, slate, or faded khaki, with some distressing or toggles at the hem. Thug Club fits the brand-name version of this well. Baggy low-rise cargos also work.

Shoes: Chunky sneakers or platform Mary Janes. Black or silver works best.

Accessories: A silver chain or studded belt worn loose on the hips, a small nylon shoulder bag, and simple silver jewelry.

Keep the colors to grey, black, off-white, and muted washes, and keep each layer thin. The look should feel a little deconstructed, not polished.

Where to Buy

Musinsa is the main Korean marketplace. It carries designer labels like Hyein Seo and Thug Club alongside cheap basics. YesStyle is good for affordable knits, boleros, and arm warmers. On Depop, search “Y2K parachute pants,” “asymmetric knit,” or “bolero shrug” to find secondhand pieces.

Realistic Budget

The record’s $100–$400 range only holds if the outfit is mostly non-designer. These are rough estimates from memory, not current prices:

Budget (about $120–$200): Use YesStyle and generic Musinsa pieces for the knit, bolero, and arm warmers, and find the pants and belt on Depop.

Mid-range (about $300–$500): Buy one designer piece, such as Thug Club pants or a Hyein Seo top, and keep everything else affordable. This is the most practical way to get the look.

Full designer (roughly $800 or more): A Hyein Seo knit and Thug Club pants alone can go past $400, before you add shoes and accessories.

Mood

For music, the record lists NewJeans, which is a natural fit, and PinkPantheress. She’s British rather than K-pop, but her Y2K-revival sound suits the aesthetic, as do the lo-fi R&B playlists the record mentions.
~~~

## 3. Opium: record without WARNING

~~~text
The Opium Outfit

The Opium look takes its name from Playboi Carti’s record label, founded in 2019, which signed Ken Carson and Destroy Lonely. So “2019 to present” is accurate. The aesthetic really took shape after Carti’s Whole Lotta Red in December 2020 and peaked around 2021–2023, when Ken Carson and Destroy Lonely brought it into the mainstream. It’s still around in 2026, though it has spread well beyond the label. The goal is to look like a vampire who shops at Rick Owens: almost all black, long and stretched-out shapes, heavy leather, and enormous shoes.

The pieces

Outerwear: A long leather trench or a heavy biker jacket is the centerpiece. Rick Owens is the main brand here; his leather jackets and long coats define the look. For less money, a vintage black leather trench from Grailed gives you most of the effect.

Top: A long, drapey tee that hangs past the hips, such as Rick Owens’ long-line tees or the cheaper DRKSHDW line. An oversized Balenciaga hoodie or a distressed, cropped graphic tee also works if you want more streetwear than gothic.

Bottoms: Distressed or waxed black denim that’s either flared or long enough to bunch up over the boots. Good options are Rick Owens DRKSHDW jeans and cargos, or Balenciaga and Vetements denim.

Footwear: This matters more than anything else. The classics are Rick Owens Kiss boots, Geobaskets, or Ramones. Balenciaga’s chunky army-style boots are the alternative. The shoes should look a little too big for the rest of you.

Accessories: The 1017 ALYX 9SM rollercoaster-buckle belt is the signature piece. Add Chrome Hearts silver if you can afford it, plus shield sunglasses and a leather or knit beanie. Matthew Williams-era Givenchy, with its padlock and chain hardware, adds the industrial touch. Williams left Givenchy in early 2024, so those pieces are now resale-only.

Where to buy

For new pieces, use SSENSE (the main source for this look; it went through a creditor-protection restructuring in 2025, so check stock and return terms), Rick Owens boutiques, and END. For resale, which is how most people actually build this wardrobe, use Grailed and Vestiaire Collective. Resale is the only route to discontinued pieces like Williams-era Givenchy.

A realistic budget

The UnderIndex range of $800–$3,500 is optimistic for a full outfit, since one Rick Owens leather jacket can cost more than the top of that range at retail. These are rough figures from memory, so expect current prices to differ.

Tier	Approach	Rough total
Entry	DRKSHDW tee and denim, used Ramones or Geobaskets, vintage leather trench, ALYX belt, all resale	$1,200–$2,000
Mid	Mix of new and used: Rick Owens boots, Balenciaga denim, used Rick Owens jacket	$3,000–$5,000
Full retail	New Rick Owens leather, Kiss boots, mainline pieces, Chrome Hearts	$8,000–$12,000+

If you’re spending carefully, put most of the money into the boots and the outerwear. People notice the shape and the shoes first, and plain black basics fill in the rest without anyone noticing.
~~~

## 4. Ura-Hara (Harajuku 90s): record without WARNING

~~~text
Ura-Hara (short for “Ura-Harajuku,” the back streets of Harajuku) has roots in the early 1990s. Hiroshi Fujiwara’s Goodenough and Jun Takahashi’s Undercover both launched around 1990, and NIGO and Takahashi opened the shop Nowhere in 1993. The scene peaked in the mid-to-late 90s and faded as a tight local movement in the early-to-mid 2000s, once brands like Bape went global. So the record’s “mid 90s – early 00s” captures the peak but starts a little late.

The outfit
Tee: A heavyweight A Bathing Ape graphic tee, like an Ape Head or college-logo print, is the cornerstone. For a punk lean, swap in an early Undercover graphic tee, since Takahashi’s punk and Sex Pistols influence was central to his early work.
Jacket: A Bape varsity jacket with a wool body, leather sleeves and chenille patches. It’s the statement piece and the biggest cost. WTAPS’s military-inspired outerwear works for a rougher look.
Denim: Relaxed straight-leg Japanese selvedge from WTAPS or Bape. Evisu is an adjacent Osaka brand from the same era, if those are hard to find. Wear it cuffed, not skinny.
Shoes: White Nike Air Force 1 lows or Dunks were the period-correct choice. Bapestas only arrived around 2002, so they suit the tail end of the era.
Accessories: A Head Porter bag (Porter’s collaboration line with Fujiwara) and a Bape camo cap or Goodenough cap finish it.

A small correction to the record: Number (N)ine was founded in 1996 and is more grunge and rock-leaning, adjacent to Ura-Hara rather than core to it. Neighborhood and Goodenough are arguably more central. On the games, Jet Set Radio fits culturally, but Shenmue doesn’t really connect to Harajuku street fashion.

Where to buy

Yahoo Auctions Japan has the deepest supply, but you’ll need a proxy service such as Buyee, ZenMarket or FromJapan. Mercari Japan works through the same proxies. Grailed and eBay are easier but pricier. In Japan, secondhand chains like 2nd Street and Ragtag are worth checking. Shipping and import taxes can add a meaningful amount, so factor them into your budget.

Fakes are everywhere, especially for Bape. Check the tag style for the era, compare against verified listings, and favor sellers with strong histories.

Realistic budget

These are rough figures from memory, and vintage prices move quickly, so check current listings.

Lean version, about $400–700: a Bape tee, non-designer Japanese selvedge, retail AF1s and a cap. Skip the varsity.
Full outfit, about $900–1,800: the jacket alone can run several hundred to around $900 depending on condition and year.
Archive grail version, $2,500 and up: early-season Undercover or key Number (N)ine pieces command collector prices.

The record’s $300–1,500 range is fair for a lean-to-mid build, but low for a fully authentic 90s head-to-toe look.
~~~

## 5. Health Goth: record without WARNING

~~~text
Health Goth Outfit

Health Goth peaked from roughly 2013 to 2016–17, so the record’s 2013–2017 window is about right. The name started as a Facebook page run by two Portland musicians, Jacob Ciocci and Mike Grabarek, before Tumblr and Instagram spread it. The fashion press covered it most heavily around 2014–15. The look treats gym clothes as something clinical, futuristic, and slightly menacing: all black (sometimes with white or reflective accents), technical fabrics, and logos used as graphic elements.

The outfit
Top: Cottweiler technical hoodie or track top. Cottweiler is the most purely Health Goth label on the list, with sterile, utilitarian sportswear that looks like a uniform. If you can’t find one, an Alexander Wang x H&M neoprene or scuba-knit sweatshirt from the 2014 collab is a classic of the era.
Layer: Nike Gyakusou running jacket. Jun Takahashi’s Undercover line for Nike gives you the technical running pieces with an arty, dark edge. A packable black shell or vest over the hoodie adds the layered, gear-heavy look.
Bottoms: black compression tights under mesh or nylon shorts. Gyakusou made both, but generic high-quality compression tights (Nike Pro, 2XU) work fine here because they’re barely branded.
Shoes: Y-3 Qasa High in triple black. This was the defining Health Goth sneaker of 2013–14. Black Nike Air Max 95s or Air Prestos are cheaper alternatives in the same spirit.
Accessories: a black athletic visor or cap, a sport-style crossbody or waist bag, and optionally a reflective strip somewhere. Alexander Wang’s 2014 H&M pieces with the oversized logo branding are good finishing details.
Where to buy

Nearly all of this is out of production, so you’ll be shopping secondhand. Grailed is best for designer pieces like Y-3, Cottweiler, and Gyakusou, and for checking authenticity. eBay often has the widest selection of Qasas and H&M collab items. Depop is good for cheaper basics and occasional lucky finds. Vestiaire Collective is worth checking too, especially from Milan, since European listings mean lower shipping costs and no import duties.

Realistic budget

These are rough resale estimates from my own knowledge, not current listings, so check prices before committing:

Piece	Approx. resale
Y-3 Qasa High	$100–250
Cottweiler hoodie / top	$100–300
Gyakusou jacket	$80–200
Shorts + compression tights	$40–120
Visor, bag, accessories	$30–100

A lean version using Air Max 95s, an H&M x Wang top, and generic tights runs about $250–350. A mid-range, mostly designer version lands around $450–700, which matches the record’s $150–700 range. Going all-in on pristine Cottweiler and Y-3 can push past $1,000.

For the full mood, the soundtrack in the record fits: Arca, Death Grips, and Gesaffelstein, with industrial techno on top.
~~~

## 6. Mall Goth: record without WARNING

~~~text
Mall Goth Outfit

Era: Mall goth peaked from roughly 1999 to 2006, the window your reference record gives. Its roots go back a few years earlier, to the late-’90s industrial and shock-rock wave, and it faded as emo and scene took over the mall around 2006–2007. It’s the commercialized, suburban cousin of trad goth: less Victorian lace and Bauhaus, more chains, straps, and nu-metal band tees, mostly bought at Hot Topic.

The outfit

Pants: Black Tripp NYC bondage or strap pants, the most important piece. Look for very wide legs, D-rings, removable straps, and chains, ideally with red, purple, or blue contrast stitching. A pair of black JNCO wide-leg jeans gives a more nu-metal, skater feel. For a skirt option, Lip Service made PVC and vinyl minis and club pieces that fit the look.

Top: A band tee, such as Slipknot, Korn, or Evanescence, all on-era. Layer a black fishnet or mesh long-sleeve underneath for the full effect.

Arms: Black-and-red or black-and-purple striped arm warmers, plus a studded leather wristband on the other wrist.

Belt: A grommet or pyramid-stud belt. Wearing two loosely crisscrossed was very common.

Neck: A cheap ball chain necklace with a plastic spiked choker or an O-ring collar.

Shoes: Chunky Demonia platform boots or creepers. Platforms with buckles are the most era-correct.

Finishing touches: A wallet chain (essential), black nail polish, and a messenger bag covered in pins and patches. Black hair with chunky red or blue streaks (Manic Panic was the standard) completes it.

Where to buy

For authentic early-2000s pieces, especially vintage Tripp, JNCO, and Lip Service, check Depop, eBay, Poshmark, and Grailed. Search terms like “Y2K Tripp NYC” or “vintage JNCO” work well. For new pieces, Hot Topic still covers band tees, arm warmers, studded belts, and cheap jewelry. Tripp NYC and JNCO have sold new pants through their own sites and retailers like Dolls Kill, and Demonia is widely sold through its own site and alternative-fashion shops. I can’t check current stock without searching, so availability may have changed.

Demonia platforms	$80–$150	$40–$80 used

Your record’s $100–$400 range is reasonable for the middle of the market. In practice, $100 only works if you thrift heavily and buy used boots. Original 2000s band tees and good-condition vintage Tripp pants can easily push you past $400. The best value is usually to spend most of the budget on the pants and boots and buy cheap new accessories.
~~~

## 7. Indie Sleaze: record without WARNING

~~~text
Indie Sleaze Outfit

Indie Sleaze belongs to roughly 2008–2012. Some people stretch it to about 2006–2013, but 2008–2012 is the peak. The idea is to look like you went straight from a Justice DJ set at 3 a.m. to brunch without going home.

The Pieces

Top: American Apparel deep V-neck tee. Go for black, heather grey, or a faded neutral. A slightly stretched-out fit is better than crisp. Buy new from American Apparel’s site for about $20–30, or find a worn-in one on Depop for $8–15.

Bottoms: pick one. Cheap Monday skinny jeans in black or grey were the standard. The brand was shut down around 2019, so look on Depop or in thrift stores, where they usually cost $20–45. For a louder night-out version, wear American Apparel metallic “disco” leggings instead, for about $30–50 new or less secondhand.

Outerwear: thrifted leather or vintage jacket. A beat-up biker jacket, a boxy blazer with the sleeves pushed up, or an oversized faux-fur coat all work. Thrift stores are your best bet here, at $30–80.

Sunglasses: Ray-Ban Wayfarers. They’re essential, even indoors and even at night. New ones run about $160–180. Vintage pairs sometimes show up on The RealReal or Depop, and a lookalike pair for $15 works fine if you’re on a budget.

Shoes: scuffed Converse Chuck Taylors or worn ankle boots. Secondhand pairs cost $15–40. The more worn they are, the better.

Beauty: smudged black eyeliner. Any drugstore kohl pencil for about $5–10 does the job. Put it on, then deliberately mess it up. Add messy hair, ideally looking like it hasn’t been washed for a day.

Budget
Thrift-heavy build: about $100–150. Buy the tee, jeans, jacket, and shoes secondhand and use lookalike sunglasses.
Full authentic build: about $250–300. Buy the American Apparel basics new and get real Ray-Bans.

Both fit the $50–$300 range typical for this aesthetic. Put your money into the sunglasses and the jacket, and save everywhere else. A slightly shabby, secondhand look is part of the style anyway.

Finishing Touches

A soundtrack of Justice, Uffie, and Crystal Castles helps set the mood. For the full effect, take your outfit photos with a point-and-shoot camera with the flash on.
~~~

## 8. Blokecore: record without WARNING

~~~text
A Classic Blokecore Outfit

When it belongs to: Blokecore as a named trend dates from about 2022 to the present. It took off on TikTok in 2022, got a boost from that winter’s World Cup, and helped drive the Adidas Samba craze of 2023. The look itself borrows from much earlier: British terrace and pub culture of the late 1980s through the early 2000s, the era of Britpop, Euro ‘96, and baggy retro kits.

The pieces
Vintage football jersey (the centerpiece): Pick a 90s or early-00s shirt with a loud design and a bold sponsor, worn untucked and slightly oversized. Good options include Umbro-era England or Manchester United, Kappa-era Roma or Barcelona, or Adidas-era kits with three stripes on the sleeves. A lesser-known club shirt reads as more authentic than an obvious big-club pick.
Straight-leg denim: Levi’s 501s in a mid or light wash, cuffed or sitting just above the trainers. Avoid skinny jeans, since a relaxed straight leg is essential to the silhouette.
Terrace trainers: Adidas Samba or Gazelle in white/black or a colorway matching the shirt. Spezials work too and are slightly more niche.
Outer layer (optional): A Kappa or Umbro track jacket, or a vintage Adidas windbreaker or half-zip, for cooler weather.
Accessories: A woolly club scarf, white or ribbed sport socks, and possibly a bucket hat or a simple chain.
Where to buy

Classic Football Shirts is the specialist for authentic vintage and retro kits, with reliable authentication and condition grading. eBay and Depop are better for bargains on shirts, 501s, and track jackets, but check photos closely, because fake vintage shirts are common. Buy Sambas and Gazelles new from Adidas or mainstream sneaker retailers, since they’re widely available again. Charity shops and vintage stores are good for cheap 501s if you can try them on.

Realistic budget (roughly $150–$400)
Item	Approx. cost
Vintage jersey	$50–$150 (rare match-worn shirts go much higher)
Levi’s 501 (secondhand / new)	$30–$100
Adidas Samba or Gazelle	$90–$120
Track jacket or windbreaker	$40–$100
Scarf, socks, accessories	$15–$40

A lean version with a jersey, jeans, and trainers comes in around $170–$250. The full kit with a jacket lands closer to $300–$400. To save money, retro reissues from brands like Score Draw or Adidas Originals cost less than true vintage and look nearly identical.

One note on your reference record: Stone Island belongs more to the casuals subculture of the 1980s and 90s, which leaned toward pricier designer pieces like Stone Island, CP Company, and Sergio Tacchini. Blokecore borrows that terrace heritage but is more about the jersey and jeans. A Stone Island jacket can work as a statement layer, but a new one costs well over $500, so it would push you past the budget above.

Soundtrack: Oasis or Blur for the Britpop heart of it, slowthai for the modern UK edge.
~~~

## 9. Quiet Luxury: record without WARNING

~~~text
A Quiet Luxury Outfit (Autumn)

Since it’s late October, I’ve built this around the cashmere overcoat and swapped the record’s linen trousers for wool flannel. Linen is the summer version of the look, and wearing it in the wrong season breaks the “effortlessly correct” feel the aesthetic depends on. Everything is in a tonal palette of camel, oatmeal, charcoal, and chocolate brown, with no visible logos.

The pieces

Overcoat: Loro Piana double-faced cashmere coat in camel. This is the anchor piece and where most of the budget goes, roughly $6,000–$9,000. Brunello Cucinelli’s cashmere coats are a comparable alternative, and a Zegna Oasi cashmere coat is a slightly less expensive route at around $4,000–$6,000.
Knit: Brunello Cucinelli cashmere turtleneck or crewneck in oatmeal. Expect about $1,500–$2,500. Loro Piana’s baby cashmere is the classic choice at a similar price. The record mentions vicuña, but a vicuña sweater typically costs $5,000–$10,000 or more on its own, so treat it as the extreme end of the look rather than a requirement.
Trousers: Zegna wool flannel trousers in charcoal, cleanly tailored with a single pleat or flat front. These run about $800–$1,300. The Row and Cucinelli both make excellent versions in the same range. Getting them hemmed properly matters more than which label you choose.
Shoes: Loro Piana Summer Walk suede loafers, or The Row’s soft leather loafers in dark brown. Roughly $900–$1,400. A simple brown suede loafer is the signature shoe of the whole trend.
Finishing touches: a plain dark leather belt with an unbranded buckle, and optionally a slim, understated dress watch on a leather strap. Skip anything with a visible monogram.

Budget

Buying everything at full retail from these brands costs about $9,000–$14,000, and most of that is the coat. The record’s “$1,500–$10,000+” range is best read as a per-piece range, not the cost of a whole outfit. To build in stages, start with the knit and the loafers, since they’re the most versatile. To capture the look for under about $1,500 total, you could combine good cashmere from Sunspel, Asket, or even Uniqlo with a well-tailored wool trouser and plain suede loafers. Fit and fabric matter more to this aesthetic than labels do. These prices are my best estimates and luxury pricing rises often, so check current listings.

Where to buy

The brands’ own boutiques and official websites give you the full range and the best alterations service. Mr Porter (menswear) and Net-a-Porter (womenswear) carry Loro Piana, Cucinelli, The Row, and Zegna, and are convenient for comparing across brands. Department stores like Saks or Harrods are another option. Secondhand sites such as The RealReal or Vestiaire Collective are worth checking for the coat in particular, because Loro Piana and Cucinelli outerwear lasts for decades and resells at a large discount.

Which years it belongs to

The record dates it from 2022 to the present, which matches the trend cycle. “Quiet luxury” became a named trend around 2022 and peaked in 2023, driven largely by HBO’s Succession and Gwyneth Paltrow’s widely discussed courtroom wardrobe. The underlying style is much older, though. It draws on old-money dressing and 1990s minimalism from designers like Jil Sander and Calvin Klein, along with Phoebe Philo’s Céline in the 2010s. Since my knowledge only runs to mid-2026, I can’t confirm how strongly the trend is still running now, but these pieces are designed to outlast trend cycles, and that’s largely the point.

If you’d like a womenswear version, The Row is especially strong there.
~~~

## 10. Whimsigoth: record without WARNING

~~~text
A Whimsigoth Outfit

Era: Whimsigoth belongs to the 1990s, roughly 1990 to 1998 as your record says. I’d stretch the end date to about 2000, since some defining touchstones landed right at the edge: The Craft (1996), Practical Magic (1998), and the early seasons of Charmed (1998). Also, American McGee’s Alice in your record came out in 2000, so it fits the mood but falls outside the stated range.

The pieces
Crushed velvet maxi or slip dress. This is the anchor. Look for deep plum, forest green, midnight blue, or black, with a bias cut or empire waist and flared or bell sleeves. Anna Sui and Betsey Johnson from the 90s are the gold standard. Anna Sui leans boho-witchy with prints and embroidery, while Betsey Johnson leans romantic and slinky.
Corset or lace-up bodice. Wear it over the dress or with a flowy skirt. Lip Service, the LA gothic and clubwear label, is the authentic 90s choice. Modern brands like Killstar or Dolls Kill copy the look well if you don’t need true vintage.
A celestial print layer. Add a sheer sun-and-moon or star-print kimono, duster, or scarf. Unbranded 90s pieces are fine here; the print matters more than the label.
Chunky silver rings. Stack several: moonstone, crescent moons, Celtic knots. Add a velvet choker or a long pendant.
Footwear. Platform boots, lace-up granny boots, or chunky Mary Janes in black.
Finishing touches. Dark berry or brown lipstick, a crushed-velvet scrunchie or hair clips, and maybe a tapestry or velvet bag.
Where to buy

Search Etsy and Depop for “90s Anna Sui,” “Betsey Johnson velvet,” “Lip Service corset,” “celestial kimono,” and “whimsigoth.” eBay and Vinted are also good for vintage designer pieces. Vintage boutiques are best for velvet, since you can check the pile and stretch in person. For jewelry, independent silversmiths on Etsy are much cheaper than vintage designer pieces.

Realistic budget

The record’s $150 to $500 range is accurate for a full outfit that mixes vintage and new:

Velvet dress: $40 to $90 unbranded vintage, or $120 to $300+ for authentic 90s Anna Sui or Betsey Johnson
Corset: $40 to $80 new, or $80 to $150 for vintage Lip Service
Celestial layer: $20 to $60
Rings and choker: $30 to $100
Boots: $60 to $150

A thrift-heavy version comes in around $150 to $200. A version built around a genuine designer dress will reach $400 to $500.

Soundtrack

To complete the mood, listen to Cocteau Twins, The Smashing Pumpkins’ Mellon Collie and the Infinite Sadness, and Stevie Nicks, who is the spiritual godmother of the look. Mazzy Star and Tori Amos also fit well.
~~~

## 11. City Boy: record without WARNING

~~~text
City Boy Outfit
~~~

## 13. Terrace Casuals: record without WARNING

~~~text
Terrace Casuals Outfit

Era: The look started around 1977–78, when Liverpool fans following their club through European Cup runs brought back continental sportswear that nobody in Britain had seen. It spread across Northern and London terraces through the early 80s and peaked in the mid-to-late 80s. By about 1990–91 acid house and Madchester had pushed most lads toward baggy jeans and loose tops. So the years are roughly 1978 to the early 90s, though the style has never really disappeared from football grounds.

The outfit (mid-to-late 80s peak)
Outerwear: C.P. Company goggle jacket. The hooded jacket with lenses built into the hood was first made in 1988 for the Mille Miglia car race, and it became the signature terrace piece. Vintage nylon or cotton versions usually sell for $300–600. A Stone Island jacket with the compass badge on the left arm is the alternative.
Knitwear: Stone Island crewneck with the compass badge. A lambswool or cotton crew in navy, olive, or a muted colour suits the era. Used ones go for about $100–220. A Lyle & Scott, Pringle, or Pringle-style Argyle jumper costs $30–80 and is just as period-correct for the earlier years.
Track top: Sergio Tacchini or Fila. The Tacchini Ghibli or Dallas and the Fila BJ (made for Björn Borg) are the classic choices. Vintage ones run $60–180, and some of these brands have reissued them for less. Wear it under the jacket, or on its own over a polo.
Polo: Lacoste or Fred Perry. A plain, well-fitting one costs $30–90 used.
Trousers: straight-leg jeans or cords. Vintage Levi’s 501s at $40–90 work, and Lois jeans and cords (a Spanish brand casuals loved) are a more knowing choice. Keep them straight and slightly cropped or turned up so the trainers show. Avoid baggy fits until about 1989.
Trainers: Adidas City Series. München, Dublin, Stockholm, and similar models have been reissued at about $90–140. Originals from the 80s are collectors’ items and are often too brittle to wear. Forest Hills, Trimm Trab, Gazelle, and Samba are also correct for the era.
Where to buy

eBay, Vinted, and Grailed are the main places to find vintage pieces, and Depop is worth checking too. Specialist terrace and casualwear resellers often sell authenticated vintage Stone Island and C.P. Company. For reissued trainers, go to Adidas directly or to sneaker shops that stock the City Series and Spezial lines.

Stone Island is one of the most faked brands on the resale market. On a genuine badge, the stitching is clean and the button holes are properly finished. Compare the labels against known genuine examples from the same era, and be suspicious of prices that seem too good. Italian sizing from the 80s also runs small, so check measurements rather than the size on the tag.

Realistic budget
Approach	What it includes	Approximate cost
Lean	Vintage Tacchini or Fila track top, reissued City Series trainers, 501s, Lyle & Scott jumper	$250–450
Mid	Add a used Stone Island knit and a Lacoste polo	$450–750
Full grail	Vintage C.P. goggle jacket, Stone Island knit, everything above	$900–1,500+

The dataset’s $150–900 range is about right for a lean-to-mid outfit. Once you add a genuine vintage goggle jacket plus Stone Island, you’ll probably go above $900.

Small corrections to the reference record

The bands are slightly mixed up across eras. The Jam were really mod revival in the late 70s, though Paul Weller is often linked to casual style. The Stone Roses and Happy Mondays belong to the very end of the period, when the baggy Madchester look was replacing the classic casual. For the late-80s outfit above, they’re the right soundtrack.
~~~

~~~text
Here’s a classic Amekaji fit built around the reproduction-heritage side of the style, the side your record describes.

When it belongs. The record’s “late 80s to present” is right for the reproduction movement. Japan’s broader love of American casual wear goes back further. Magazines like Popeye and the “Heavy Duty” look were pushing American workwear and outdoor gear in the mid-1970s. The repro boom took off in the late 80s and 90s, when brands like Studio D’Artisan (founded 1979) and the other “Osaka Five” denim makers began rebuilding vintage Levi’s on old shuttle looms. It’s still going strong today, so it’s a living style rather than a period costume.

The outfit

Jeans: Studio D’Artisan or Iron Heart, about $250–$400. Studio D’Artisan is the purist choice, with slubby selvedge in a 1950s-style straight leg. Iron Heart is known for very heavy denim, often 21oz, that fades hard and lasts for years. Buy them raw, wear them a lot, and cuff them once over the boots.

Sweatshirt: The Real McCoy’s loopwheel crewneck, about $180–$280. It’s knit on slow tubular loopwheel machines, so there are no side seams. Grey marl with a V-insert at the neck is the classic version. Warehouse and Loopwheeler make excellent alternatives.

Jacket: Buzz Rickson’s MA-1 or a Real McCoy’s A-2. The Buzz Rickson’s nylon MA-1 costs about $500–$750 and is the easier entry point, good for October weather. A horsehide A-2 from Real McCoy’s is the grail piece, usually $1,500 or more.

Boots: Engineer boots, about $700–$1,200. For a Japanese-made option, Buco is the Real McCoy’s boot line, and Clinch is a cult favorite. Wesco is the American original many repro fans wear. Brown or black leather both work.

Accent: one Kapital piece, about $100–$300. Kapital is more eclectic and patchwork-heavy than strict repro, so use it as seasoning, not the base. A bandana, belt, or pair of boro-style socks adds character without breaking the look.

Bought new, a sensible version (jeans, sweatshirt, MA-1, boots) runs roughly $1,600–$2,600. With an A-2 and Clinch boots you’re looking at $4,000 or more. Where to buy

Official brand sites are best for current stock and sizing charts, though shipping and import duties can add a lot. Yahoo Auctions Japan has the deepest selection of used pieces. You’ll need a proxy service such as Buyee or ZenMarket, and the fees add up. Grailed is the easiest secondhand option outside Japan. Specialist heritage shops like Self Edge, Blue in Green, and Clutch Cafe also stock several of these brands and can help with fit.

These prices are from my own knowledge rather than a live check. Exchange rates and import costs shift, so confirm current prices before budgeting precisely. One sizing tip: Japanese brands often run small, so check measurements in centimeters rather than trusting the tag.
~~~
